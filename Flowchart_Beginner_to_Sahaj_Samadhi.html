The Three Pillars (3Ds)
Do NOT rely on physical practice alone. "Those who attempt to subdue the mind by bodily contortions strive as vainly as those who wish to bind a mad elephant with a rope of grass."
Do NOT surrender to fatalism. "Nothing comes from what the dull and mistaken call chance or fate." Present effort is stronger than past karma.
Do NOT mistake outer renunciation for inner liberation. Sikhidhwaja spent 18 years as a hermit with zero progress — ego intact.
Do NOT suppress the mind violently. It must be trained like a child — not forced. Suppression strengthens the suppressor (the ego).
Do NOT mistake the map for the territory. "Scriptures are not the means to divine knowledge." Reading is not liberation — recognising yourself is.

The Core Formula — Vasishta's Four Sentinels of Liberation
Santosha
Contentment — deep satisfaction with what is; not perpetually hungry for more
Vichara
Self-inquiry — "Who am I?" turned toward the source, not toward an answer in words
Shama
Tranquility of mind — inner composure; undisturbed by the world's fluctuations
Satsanga
Company of the wise — osmosis of truth; the teacher's presence mirrors what the student already is

"Practice any ONE of these four virtues and the others follow." — Vasishta

The Awakening · Before Stage 1
You Are Trapped — And You See It
Vairagya Arising · The First Light of Wisdom

You suddenly see it clearly. Pleasure is temporary. Achievements fade. Relationships end in parting. Even the best the world offers — wealth, fame, love, power — leaves you hungry again. This is not depression. This is not failure. This is vairagya — the first awakening of genuine discrimination.

"I have examined everything within the range of worldly experience and found nothing that truly satisfies. My mind finds no rest — yet I can no longer be deceived."

— Rama, opening Yoga Vasishta
What Is Happening Inside You
  • A creeping sense that no worldly gain brings lasting peace — pleasures deliver, then immediately create new hunger
  • Repetitive suffering begins to feel meaningless rather than motivating
  • Questions arise: Why am I doing all this? Is there something more?
  • The world's promises begin to feel hollow — not through force, but through seeing clearly
  • Even heaven is not enough — King Arishtanemi refused Indra's paradise because it was temporary
Pitfalls
  • Mistaking this for depression. Vairagya is clarity, not collapse.
  • Swinging back into worldly pursuit to escape the discomfort of questioning.
  • Nihilism — concluding "nothing matters" rather than "something deeper matters."

"A place from which one must eventually fall is no better than this world. I do not seek heaven. I seek liberation — the state from which there is no return."

— King Arishtanemi to Indra's messenger
Sign You Are Ready for Stage 1

When the question "Is there something beyond all this?" becomes genuine and persistent — not a passing thought, but a quiet insistence that will not leave.

Stage 1 · First Step on the Ladder
Subheccha — Good Will
The First Genuine Desire for Liberation

The desire for liberation is born — not as abstract philosophy but as a felt need. Something in you turns toward the light. Vasishta says this stage is like a tender sprout — it must be watered daily with reason, tended carefully, never forced.

"Why do I sit idle? I must know the scriptures in the company of good men."

— Vasishta describing the inner speech of Stage 1
What Is Happening Inside You
  • You genuinely want to understand the nature of reality — not just as a hobby
  • Attraction toward spiritual texts, teachers, or communities arises naturally
  • You begin to distinguish actions that increase suffering from those that reduce it
  • A preference for peace over stimulation quietly develops
The Four Sentinels — Pick ONE to Begin

Practice any ONE and the others follow. Choose the one most natural to you right now.

Santosha — Contentment
Each morning, before tasks: sit 2 minutes and notice what is already present and sufficient. Not gratitude as technique — genuine noticing. What is already here, already enough?
Vichara — Self-Inquiry
When something causes suffering today, ask — "Who is suffering?" Not to answer in words, but to turn attention toward the one who suffers. Even 30 seconds daily is the beginning.
Shama — Cultivating Peace
Each day, find one moment where you deliberately do not react. An irritation arises — pause. In that pause, notice what is present before the reaction. That noticing is shama in seed form.
Satsanga — Seek Good Company
Find one person, community, or text genuinely pointing toward truth and spend time with it weekly. "Except by the light of scriptures and association with the wise, there is no way to cross the river of ignorance."
Additional Practices
Contemplate impermanence — 5 minutes daily
Call to mind something you value or fear losing. Hold it quietly. Acknowledge: this will change. It always was changing. Vairagya grows naturally when you see clearly — it need not be forced.
Observe one desire without acting on it
Each day, watch one desire arise — urge to check phone, to snack, to say the clever thing — and simply watch it. Don't suppress, don't follow. Notice it arose, intensified, and what happened when you didn't feed it.
Read the Yoga Vasishta — starting with the stories
"This excellent work is to be read always." Start with the stories — understanding comes through narrative first. The stories plant seeds below the intellect. King Lavana, Queen Leela, Shukadeva.
Pitfalls
  • Performing spirituality — appearing to seek while actually seeking status or identity in a new tribe
  • Reading without reflection — accumulating spiritual concepts as intellectual decoration
  • Jumping to advanced practices before this foundation is stable
What This Stage Feels Like

"The venerable nature of yogis germinates in the first stage, blossoms in the second, and becomes fruitful in the third." — Vasishta. A tender sprout. Fragile. Easily crushed by cynicism. But genuinely alive.

Sign You Are Ready for Stage 2

When study and good company naturally produce disgust with worldliness — when the world's promises feel genuinely hollow, not just intellectually. When you prefer a quiet evening of reflection over entertainment without forcing it.

Stage 2 · Investigation
Vicharana — Right Inquiry
Systematic Discernment · Active Investigation

Systematic investigation begins. You actively examine the nature of mind, world, and Self — testing what you study against direct experience. Pride, vanity and greed begin to be shed "like a snake casting off its old skin."

"He takes off his outer habit of pride and vanity, his jealousy and greed — like a snake casting off an old skin. Having thus purified his mind, he attends to the service of his spiritual teachers."

— Vasishta on Stage 2
What Is Happening Inside You
  • You begin to distinguish the real from the unreal in direct experience
  • Pride, vanity, jealousy and greed are now visible — you can watch them arise rather than being identified with them
  • The question "Who is experiencing all this?" starts to arise naturally
  • Actions that cloud the mind become naturally unattractive; clarity becomes preferable
Practices
Formal self-inquiry (Vichara) — 15 minutes daily
Sit quietly. Ask: "Who am I?" — not as a riddle to solve with words, but as a direction of attention. When a thought arises, ask: who is thinking this? When an emotion arises: who is feeling this? Don't seek an answer — seek the one who is asking. Stay with the turning-toward rather than reaching for a conclusion.
Begin pranayama — 10 minutes before meditation
Breathe in (puraka) for 4 counts → hold (kumbhaka) for 4 counts → breathe out (rechaka) for 8 counts. "As flying dust settles when wind stops, flying thoughts settle when breathing slows — they being one and the same thing." Pranayama is support, not the path itself.
Karma Yoga — "Am I the doer?"
In any significant action today, hold this question while acting fully: "Is this happening through me, or am I doing it?" The Yoga Vasishta: "An indifferent action, like a fried grain, does not germinate into binding effect."
Service without seeking recognition
Perform one act daily with no expectation of gratitude or reciprocity. Then notice: what arises in the mind when the expected reward doesn't come? The noticing is the practice.
Critical Pitfall
  • Intellectualising. "Ignorance is always ignorant of truth — even during meditation. Knowledge is always knowing — even during sleep." Frameworks are not Self-knowledge. Verify in direct experience.
  • Seeking special experiences — the path is not about peak states. It is about seeing clearly in ordinary moments.
Sign You Are Ready for Stage 3

When the mind spontaneously turns away from worldly amusements — not through discipline but through genuine disinterest. When solitude feels more natural than stimulation.

Stage 3 · The Thinning
Tanumanasa — The Mind Grows Thin
Genuine Detachment · Two Kinds of Disconnection

The mind's compulsive grip on sense objects weakens. Not through suppression — through seeing. Two distinct layers of inner detachment emerge: ordinary disconnection (not attached to outcomes) and absolute disconnection (not identified with the one who could be attached).

"The flower of inner discrimination is expanded, like the lotus-bud, by the sunbeams of reason — and produces the fruit of renunciation in the garden of the third stage."

— Vasishta
What Is Happening Inside You
  • Solitude becomes genuinely pleasurable — not something to escape
  • Two kinds of disconnection begin to manifest (see below)
  • The mind begins to thin: turbulence less frequent, grip less total
  • You begin to perceive that it is not "I" acting but something acting through this form
The Two Disconnections
Ordinary Disconnection

"I have no ultimate control over outcomes. All prosperity and adversity, health and disease happen to me of their own accord. All union is for separation, all gain is for loss." Frees from anxiety about results without yet dissolving the sense of self.

Absolute Disconnection

"It is not I but the great Consciousness that acts through this form. Having left aside the thought of one's agency — whoever sits quiet and tranquil in themselves — such a one is absolutely unconnected with everything." The ego itself is seen through.

Practices
Daily meditation — minimum 20–30 minutes, without exception
The practice is not to have no thoughts but to not be swept away by them. Watch the mind the way you watch clouds — they appear, move, dissolve. You are the sky in which they appear. Return to this recognition every time you drift.
The three-state inquiry
Throughout the day, notice: in waking — "who is watching this experience?" At the moment of falling asleep — "what is still present as the body relaxes?" On waking — "what was here before the first thought arose?" This trains turiya-awareness — the witness present in all three states.
Equal vision (Samadarsana)
Look at something you consider ugly. Look at something beautiful. Ask: is the Consciousness in which both appear any different? The same awareness holds both. Rest in the awareness rather than in the preference.
Pitfalls
  • Performing non-attachment while secretly attached — the ego learns the language of liberation
  • Stopping here. Stage 3 can feel like a comfortable plateau. The ego can settle here very happily, mistaking calm for liberation.
Sign You Are Ready for Stage 4

When the world begins to appear genuinely dream-like during waking — not as a concept but as a spontaneous perception. Duality starts to feel like a trick of the mind rather than an absolute fact.

Stage 4 · The Threshold
Sattwapatti — Resting in the Self
The Gateway · Beginning of Liberation

The threshold of liberation. The world's apparent solidity begins to thin as a direct perception — not a philosophy. Unity shines where duality had appeared. Stages 1–3 were the waking-state of the yogi. Stage 4 is "dreaming" — the solidity of the world's separateness dissolves like clouds in autumn.

"The yogi in this state is said to have reached the fourth stage when he sees the world like a vision in a dream. As the mistake of duality disappears and the knowledge of unity shines supremely bright."

— Vasishta
What Is Happening Inside You
  • The world appears genuinely dream-like — a perceptual shift, not a concept applied
  • The sense of separate things begins to thin; objects are seen as appearances rather than solid independent entities
  • The Divine is now the actual refuge — worldly supports seen as secondary and temporary
  • "Resting in the fourth and succeeding stages is called liberation" — this is the entry point
Practices
Turiya meditation — pure witness practice
Sit. Close eyes. Notice thoughts arising and passing. Notice feelings. Notice sensations. Now: what is the thing that notices all of this? Don't think about it — stay as it. Not a thought about the witness but the witnessing itself. Even one minute of genuine resting as the witness is worth more than an hour of concentration practice.
Hold "Who am I?" in stillness — not in thought
Vasishta on turiya: "that state in which the feelings of one's egoism and non-egoism are utterly drowned under total aloofness — settled in one unchangeable uniform course of tranquility." Aim for this even briefly. The question dissolves the questioner when held in stillness rather than in thought.
Service as complete offering
Bhagiratha's liberation came through total selfless engagement — not through withdrawal. Act fully. Offer completely. Claim nothing. The liberating recognition is not "I should not claim this" but "there is genuinely no one here to claim it."
The Ego's Most Sophisticated Trap
  • Identifying with being "the witness." The ego creates a new subtle identity: "I am the one who watches." Turiya is not an object within consciousness — it IS consciousness. There is no "I" that witnesses. There is only witnessing.
  • Mistaking temporary bliss states for liberation. Stage 4 is the threshold, not the completion. Beautiful experiences arise and pass. Liberation is what remains when the peak experience has passed.
Sign You Are Ready for Stage 5

When even the distinction between "I am in samadhi" and "I am not in samadhi" begins to dissolve. When profound stillness is easier to sustain than it is to leave.

Stage 5 · Deep Absorption
Asamsakti — Non-Attachment
The Sleeping State of Yoga · Undivided Unity

Profound absorption. The sense of duality melts. The yogi rests in awareness of undivided unity. Described as the "sleeping state of yoga" — not unconscious, but deeply, effortlessly still. Inner delight entirely self-sufficient. Nothing outside is needed to feel whole.

"The sense of duality is entirely melted down and lost in the cheerfulness of an awakened mind. The yogi loses all external perceptions and sits quietly with internal vision."

— Vasishta
What Is Happening Inside You
  • External perceptions no longer pull the attention compulsively — the world appears but does not hook
  • The sense of duality is "entirely melted down" — not as concept but as actual texture of experience
  • Inner delight is self-sufficient — nothing outside is needed to feel complete
  • The yogi "sits quietly with internal vision" — appears asleep to others, profoundly alive internally
Practices
Extended silent meditation — 1 hour or more
Not forcing — simply remaining as the witness, allowing the recognition to deepen on its own. At this stage, formal meditation is less a technique than deliberate resting in what is always already the case.
Nididhyasana — prolonged contemplation
Take one sentence: "There is only pure Consciousness." Sit with it for 45 minutes. Not analyzing. Not picturing. Simply letting the recognition saturate the mind the way water saturates cloth — resting in the direction it points.
Pitfalls
  • Attachment to the bliss of Stage 5 — creating subtle craving for the absorption state
  • Premature withdrawal from responsibilities. Vishnu woke Prahlada from samadhi because "your duties are not finished." Liberation does not end responsibility — it transforms it.
Sign You Are Ready for Stage 6

When even consciousness of being "in" or "out" of the absorbed state dissolves — when there is simply this, always. When the question of spiritual progress no longer arises because there is no longer a "you" anxiously tracking it.

Stage 6 · Living Liberation
Padarthabhavana — Jivan Mukti
The Liberated Sage in the World · Freed While Living

Living Liberation. The ego's grip is completely dissolved. The sage functions in the world normally — governing, teaching, loving, fighting — yet nothing sticks. This is the state of Janaka, Bali, Prahlada, Chudala. "Extinct or dead to consciousness" yet fully alive. "Like a lamp in a picture that emits no flame."

"The yogi remains unmindful of everything, unconscious of unity or duality. Within and without full of divine ecstasy, like a full pot in a sea. He is possessed of higher power, yet appears as worthless on the outside."

— Vasishta
Portrait of the Living Liberated Sage
Always as deep and clear as a large lake in a valley
Ever content without expectation of reward for actions
Not moved by torture or pleasure of the body
Neither praises nor is displeased when honored or dishonored
Like a crystal — takes no tinge from effects of actions
Hurts nobody and is not hurt by any
Free from anger, affection, fear, and ego-reactions
Acts from prior momentum like a potter's wheel
Not terrified by warfare, thunder, demons, or death
May follow social customs or not — indifferent either way
Can die anywhere — holy place or savage's hut — irrelevant
Sees Brahman in all — tree, demon, god, grain of dust

"Whether engaged in business or retired from it, whether living with family or leading a single life — the man who thinks himself as nothing but consciousness, who has nothing to fear or care or be sorry for, is reckoned as liberated in this life."

— Manu to King Ikshaku
Transition to Stage 7

There is no effort here. Stage 7 is not attained — it is what Stage 6 opens into when even the subtlest residue of "I am the liberated one" dissolves.

Stage 7 · The Arrival
Sahaj Samadhi — The Natural State
Turyaga · Permanent Liberation · The Potter's Wheel

Natural, permanent, effortless liberation. The samadhi that coexists with all activity. Not a state entered and exited. Not a peak experience. The permanent ground of every moment, every action, every breath. Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva appear to Rama: "Remain firmly established in Brahman always. Joyfully carry out your work."

"Ah! I have attained the most wonderful state of nirvana — the end of the purpose of life! I am always in form, but in the extremely peaceful myself there is nothing. There is nothing now covetable to me."

— Rama's cry of liberation
Ordinary vs. Sahaj Samadhi
Ordinary samadhi
Requires withdrawal from activity — the mind becomes still by retreating. Returning to activity disturbs it. This is not sahaj samadhi.
Sahaj samadhi — the goal
"The rishis, having attained nirvana, are endowed with the highest state of sahaj samadhi that allows them to mix with people and perform their allotted duties in life without ever coming down from their samadhi state." Permanent. Effortless. Coexisting with full worldly engagement.
What Is Now the Case
  • The samadhi coexists with all activity — governing, teaching, loving, fighting, eating, sleeping
  • Actions continue from prior vasanas — the potter's wheel spins without new force applied
  • "The mind withdrawn from its functions, freed from all thoughts, all doubts drowned in even temperament"
  • Full, free, unclaimed engagement with life — Rama returns to fulfil his destiny as king
  • The gods confirm: act fully, from Brahman — every act is worship, every moment liberation
The Final Teaching

"This is neither the state of waking, owing to its lack of any wish or concern, nor the state of sound sleep. It is that calmness in which the wise man sees everything going on in the world — and is moved by none of it."

He is the knower, the knowledge, and all that is to be known.
He is the seer, the act of seeing, and all that is to be seen.
— Opening verse, Yoga Vasishta
